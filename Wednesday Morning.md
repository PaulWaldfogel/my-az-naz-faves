📖 Sermon Outline: "A Tale of Two Prophets"

**Speaker:** Dr. Shauna Gaines ([34:45](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=b-7b8QgQqr8&t=2085s))  
**Scripture Focus:** Ezekiel 11:16–20 ([38:01](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=b-7b8QgQqr8&t=2281s))

1. Two Parallel Lives

Dr. Gaines highlighted the structural similarities between two historical prophets: **Ezekiel**, the Old Testament priest-turned-prophet in Babylonian exile, and **Mildred Bangs Wynkoop**, a cornerstone 20th-century theologian in the Church of the Nazarene ([38:12](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=b-7b8QgQqr8&t=2292s)). Both individuals faced a massive structural "crisis of faith" in their communities, stretching them to uncover a truer, larger vision of God's holiness ([44:27](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=b-7b8QgQqr8&t=2667s)).

2. The Trap of Rigid Formulas

- **Ezekiel's Context:** Raised in Jerusalem's elite Zadokite priestly line, Ezekiel watched institutional rituals function like clockwork while the community suffered from an underlying sickness of idolatry (divided hearts) and systemic injustice ([45:44](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=b-7b8QgQqr8&t=2744s)). People treated institutional rituals like a formula to safely "evade sin" rather than change inwardly ([53:49](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=b-7b8QgQqr8&t=3229s)).
- **Mildred Wynkoop's Context:** Raised in an authentic ecosystem of grace that famously saved her alcoholic grandfather, she later grew deeply disillusioned by institutional legalism ([49:13](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=b-7b8QgQqr8&t=2953s)). She realized that global church altars were producing a spiritual "assembly line" where people spoke of sinlessness but clear credibility gaps remained between their spoken piety and daily horizontal behaviors ([54:58](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=b-7b8QgQqr8&t=3298s)).

3. Relational Holiness in Exile

Dragged to far-off foreign spaces (Ezekiel to Babylon; Wynkoop to a missionary teaching position in Japan), both leaders were forced to look past hollow checked boxes ([56:46](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=b-7b8QgQqr8&t=3406s)). Through Ezekiel 11, God revealed that holiness is not about external scapegoats but about replacing a heart of stone with a soft, undivided heart of flesh ([39:52](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=b-7b8QgQqr8&t=2392s)). Wynkoop penned _A Theology of Love_, clarifying that sin is an absence of holiness, and true holiness is a dynamic, growing, relational love with Jesus Christ rather than a static moral checklist ([1:00:27](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=b-7b8QgQqr8&t=3627s)).

> **Key Takeaway:** We cannot artificially fabricate a spiritual movement on our own ([1:16:58](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=b-7b8QgQqr8&t=4618s)). If we want a truer manifestation of holiness, we must begin with complete transparency—authentically admitting when our bones are dry so that God can breathe new life into them ([1:17:56](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=b-7b8QgQqr8&t=4676s)).
